# Weekly Structure and Session Progression

How many runs a week, which ones are quality, and in which order the session types enter the plan. This overrides the generic weekly template of `coaches-guide.md` (section 4) where they differ. Paces, HR and per-session limits are in `.claude/skills/running-coach/references/pace-and-hr.md`.

## 1. Runs per week follow the volume

Start with **3 runs a week**. Add a day only when the weekly volume needs it, not before:

| Weekly volume | Runs per week |
|---|---|
| up to ~30-35 km | 3 |
| from ~30-35 km | 4 |
| from ~40-45 km | 5 |

These thresholds are indicative: adapt them to the runner (age, history, injuries, time available, how they recover). The trigger is that the volume can no longer grow safely on the existing days (session limits in `pace-and-hr.md`, section 4), not the number itself.

Rest days are the easy part of the week: with 3 runs and 4 rest days there is no need for an easy run to recover.

## 2. Which runs are quality

**Default: every run is a quality session.** With 3 runs:
- **Long run**
- **Threshold** (Tempo continuo or Tempo a ripetute)
- **Reps** (Repetition or Interval)

**If the runner does not tolerate 3 quality sessions**, replace one with an easy run. The week becomes:
- Long run
- Threshold **or** reps (alternating week by week, or as the phase requires)
- Easy run

**When a day is added (4th, 5th):**
- if the runner already does 3 quality sessions, the new day is an **easy run**;
- if the runner is on 2 quality sessions, the new day depends on what they tolerate: easy at first, a quality session again only once they handle it.

The same rule applies to the 5th day.

**Signs of not tolerating the load** (judge over 1-2 weeks, not one session):
- sessions not completed, or paces not held at the usual HR;
- resting HR above its 7-day range or HRV low for several days;
- heavy legs or RPE higher than expected that persist into the next session;
- niggles or pain that come back.

Before cutting a quality session, try the lighter fixes: a recovery week, a shorter session, the slower half of the pace range.

## 3. Order in which session types enter the plan

Each step starts only when the previous one is handled well.

1. **Base**: easy running only, at least 30 minutes, at conversational pace, walking when needed (beginners, and anyone returning after a break of 3-4 weeks or more: see `pace-and-hr.md`, section 2).
2. **Strides**: short relaxed accelerations (60-100 m) after an easy run, once the runner runs 30 minutes continuously.
3. **Threshold**, first as **Tempo a ripetute** (cruise intervals: short reps at T pace with short recoveries), then as **Tempo continuo** (a continuous block, grown over the weeks).
4. **Repetition** (short reps, 200-400 m at R pace, full recovery).
5. **Interval** (800 m and longer at I pace, reps of 3-5 minutes), starting from the shortest reps.

The long run grows alongside, from the base phase on.

A runner with a training history (case b or c in `pace-and-hr.md`) can start further along the sequence, at the step their recent training supports.
